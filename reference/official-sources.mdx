Nexus Ciencia is built on a foundation of primary, government-published information. Every factual claim in the portal — from Orion capsule recovery procedures to extremophile biology in the Mariana Trench — traces back to one of three official sources. This page lists those sources, explains why each was selected, and provides direct links to the relevant sections of each website.

## Why These Three Sources?

The selection of exactly these three official sources reflects the three knowledge domains that Nexus Ciencia brings together:

### NASA — The Space Domain

NASA publishes mission data, engineering specifications, and program timelines directly from the agency responsible for Artemis. Using nasa.gov ensures that all information about the Orion capsule, SLS, crew compositions, and launch schedules reflects official, version-controlled public releases rather than secondary interpretations.

### U.S. Navy — The Military Operations Domain

The Navy's formal role in astronaut recovery operations is not incidental — it is contractual and operationally critical. navy.mil is the authoritative source for understanding the ships, divers, helicopters, and protocols that make every Artemis splashdown survivable. It also provides the historical record for Mercury, Gemini, and Apollo recoveries, and for Navy programs like Project Vanguard and the NRL's contributions to the founding of NASA Goddard.

### NOAA Ocean Explorer — The Ocean Science Domain

NOAA Ocean Explorer bridges the gap between space exploration and deep-sea science. The same extreme-environment engineering challenges that appear in bathyscaphe design and deep-ocean research reappear in spacecraft design; the same extremophile organisms that live in total darkness at 11,000 meters inform astrobiology models for subsurface oceans on Europa and Enceladus. NOAA's peer-reviewed, government-published research makes it the appropriate citation for all ocean and life-science content in the portal.
