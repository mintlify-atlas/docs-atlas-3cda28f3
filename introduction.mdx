Nexus Ciencia is an interactive, browser-based educational portal subtitled *"Del Abismo Marino al Espacio Profundo"* ("From the Marine Abyss to Deep Space"). This introduction covers what the portal is, the four feature areas it provides, the four core scientific topics it teaches, and everything you need to know before your first visit.

<Note>
  The Nexus Ciencia portal itself is entirely in **Spanish**. All on-screen labels, articles, game prompts, and AI responses are written in Spanish. This documentation site is in **English** to help a wider audience understand and navigate the project.
</Note>

## What Is Nexus Ciencia?

Nexus Ciencia is a single-page web application that runs entirely in the browser — no installation, no account, and no login required. It was built to make the real, often-overlooked relationship between **NASA** and the **U.S. Navy** accessible and engaging for Spanish-speaking audiences.

The portal blends long-form science journalism, an integrated AI assistant, interactive arcade simulations, and a historical timeline into one cohesive experience. Visitors can jump between reading detailed scientific analyses, chatting with the built-in AI, and piloting a rocket or deep-sea submersible — all without leaving the page.

## Four Scientific Topics Covered

The knowledge base section (`#explicaciones`) organises its content around four interconnected themes:

| Topic | Summary |
|---|---|
| **NASA–Navy Origins** | How the U.S. Navy's Project Vanguard and the Naval Research Laboratory laid the technical and human foundations that became NASA's Goddard Space Flight Center after the agency's creation in 1958. |
| **Astronaut Rescue at Sea** | Why every U.S. crewed capsule (Mercury, Apollo, and today's Orion) splashes down in the ocean — and how the Navy deploys amphibious ships, MH-60S helicopters, and Navy SEAL divers to recover astronauts safely. |
| **Underwater Space Training (NEEMO)** | How NASA's *Extreme Environment Mission Operations* program sends astronauts to live aboard the *Aquarius* underwater habitat, 19 metres below the surface off Florida, to rehearse spacewalks and isolation protocols in conditions that mirror the Moon and Mars. |
| **Mariana Trench Technology** | How the engineering required to survive the crushing pressures of the deepest point on Earth (nearly 11,000 m) directly informs the instruments NASA is designing to search for life in the subsurface oceans of Jupiter's moon Europa and Saturn's moon Enceladus. |

## No Install, No Login

Because Nexus Ciencia is delivered as a single `.html` file, it requires nothing beyond a modern web browser. There are no dependencies to install, no server to configure, and no account to create. Open the file locally or serve it from any static host and it works immediately.
